# Founding Engineer Hiring Benchmarks (US + India Remote)

Date: 2026-04-11
Owner: ATLAS
Confidence: Medium-High on US cash/equity and hiring-process guidance; Medium on India cash benchmarks

## Context

Thoughtseed's active engineering plan defines the first hire as a broad-surface founding engineer who stabilizes the Paperclip-backed product spine, ships across backend and frontend boundaries, and helps establish release discipline and technical judgment inside a still founder-shaped environment.[1] That role scope is materially broader than a generic senior software engineer seat, so compensation and evaluation should be benchmarked against early startup founding-engineer expectations rather than against a standard IC3/IC4 market average alone.[1]

## Scope And Method

This brief combines:

- first-party startup compensation data from Pave and Carta for US venture-backed hiring.[2][3]
- current market aggregates from Levels.fyi for US and India senior software engineers.[4][5]
- startup hiring-funnel data from Ashby and LinkedIn for sourcing-channel and interview-process decisions.[6][7][8]
- public-sector hiring guidance and selection-research evidence for interview design.[9][10][11]
- live salary-transparent remote job examples for India-eligible senior engineering roles.[12][13]

The purpose is not to produce one false-precision number. It is to define a defensible hiring band, the right sourcing stack, and a low-noise interview loop for a US + India remote search.

## Evidence

### Compensation Benchmarks

#### United States

1. Pave's 2025 founding-engineer benchmark for venture-backed, pre-seed and seed companies in tier-1 US markets places senior software-engineer cash compensation between roughly `$187,000` at the 50th percentile and `$235,000` at the 90th percentile, with the displayed market maximum at `$250,000`. Equity runs from about `0.33%` at the 50th percentile to `1.24%` at the 90th percentile, with the displayed market maximum at `1.32%`.[2]
2. Carta's H1 2025 startup compensation report says engineering is tied with product as the highest-paid new-hire function at startups, with average new-hire salary at `$189,000` in June 2025.[3]
3. Levels.fyi's current US senior software-engineer market page shows median total compensation at `$246,400`, with the 25th percentile at `$175,200` and the 75th percentile at `$353,500`.[4]

#### India

1. Levels.fyi's India senior software-engineer market page shows median total compensation at `Rs 4,878,856`, with the 25th percentile at `Rs 3,190,188`, the 75th percentile at `Rs 7,275,154`, and the 90th percentile at `Rs 9,892,794`.[5]
2. Remote.com's indexed posting for a senior software engineer role at SupplyHouse, remote and India-only, lists pay at `$35,000-$40,000` per year.[12]
3. Remote.com's indexed posting for a senior frontend React engineer role at ParcelHero, remote and India-only, lists pay at `Rs 150,000-Rs 200,000` per month.[13]

#### What The Range Conflict Means

US startup-specific founding-engineer data is much cleaner than India-specific founding-engineer data. India's public market is easier to observe at the generic senior-engineer level than at the founding-engineer title level. That means the India recommendation below is a synthesis of senior-market benchmarks plus live remote-posting examples, not a single canonical founding-engineer dataset.[5][12][13]

### Sourcing-Channel Benchmarks

1. Ashby's 2025 startup hiring report says the largest share of hires in venture-backed startups still comes from inbound candidates, followed by sourced candidates. It also finds that the smallest startups get `30%` of hires from sourcing, that remote roles receive `42%` more inbound applications than hybrid/on-site roles, and that remote technical roles see `13%` higher offer-acceptance rates.[6]
2. Ashby's 2025 referrals report, based on more than 38 million applications, says referrals make up less than `1%` of applications but convert at `40%` from application to interview and `16%` from interview to offer.[7]
3. LinkedIn's 2025 skills-first hiring analysis says companies with the most skills-based searches are `12%` more likely to make a quality hire and `33%` more likely to hire people in the top quartile of recruiter demand.[8]

### Interview-Loop Evidence

1. Ashby reports that hired startup candidates spend roughly `2.5-3` hours interviewing, and that startups with fewer than 25 employees can reduce time to hire by almost `30%` when a recruiter is involved early.[6]
2. The US Office of Personnel Management says structured interviews increase validity and reliability, improve interviewer agreement, and help reduce bias because candidates are assessed on the same job-related criteria.[9]
3. OPM's assessment guide says work samples and simulations are strongest when applicants are expected to arrive with the required skills already in place, because the task directly mirrors job content and improves content validity.[10]
4. Schmidt and Hunter's meta-analysis reports that two of the highest-validity combinations are general mental ability plus work samples (`.63`) and general mental ability plus structured interviews (`.63`).[11]

## Analysis

### Facts

1. Thoughtseed's first engineering seat is explicitly broader than a narrow implementation role; it is part execution engine, part technical decision partner.[1]
2. In the US, startup-specific first-engineer cash benchmarks cluster in the high-`$100k`s to low-`$200k`s, with meaningful equity measured in fractions of a percent rather than token grants.[2][3]
3. In India, the observable senior-engineer market centers materially lower in cash than US startup founding-engineer data, but top-quartile and top-decile India senior compensation is still substantial by local standards.[5]
4. Referrals and sourced outreach are much more efficient than generic inbound on a per-candidate basis, even if inbound remains highest-volume.[6][7]
5. Skills-first search criteria, structured interviews, and real work samples are all better-supported by evidence than pedigree filters or purely conversational interview loops.[8][9][10][11]

### Interpretation

1. Thoughtseed should not price this role like a generic India senior engineer if it expects the person to absorb founding-level ambiguity, write across the stack, and co-shape technical operating rhythm. That expectation comes from the internal role scope, not from the title alone.[1] This is ATLAS analysis.
2. US and India should share the same hiring bar and scorecard, but not the same cash band. Cash should stay location-aware. Equity should absorb part of the difference when cash is discounted materially below the US benchmark. This is ATLAS analysis grounded in the market evidence above.
3. Remote should be the default search posture unless there is a hard operational reason not to use it. The data points toward a larger top-of-funnel and better technical offer acceptance for remote roles.[6] This is ATLAS analysis.

## Recommendations

### Recommended Compensation Bands

#### US Remote

- Recommended target base: `$190,000-$235,000`
- Stretch band for unusually strong candidates: up to `$250,000`
- Recommended equity band: `0.5%-1.25%`

Rationale: this keeps Thoughtseed inside the observed founding-engineer and startup-engineering market while preserving room to pay above median when a candidate can function as both hands-on builder and technical partner.[2][3][4]

#### India Remote

- Recommended target annual cash-equivalent: `Rs 3.5M-Rs 7.5M`
- Stretch band for rare cross-border-caliber candidates: toward `Rs 9M+`
- Recommended equity band: `0.25%-0.9%`

Rationale: the lower end clears the practical floor suggested by transparent India-remote senior openings, while the upper end reaches into the upper quartile of India senior-engineer market compensation. If Thoughtseed wants a true founding-caliber operator in India at a cash band below the top quartile, equity should move toward the upper end of the range. This is ATLAS synthesis from market evidence, not a direct published benchmark.[5][12][13]

### Recommended Sourcing Stack

1. **Referrals first.**
   Build the first 20-30 candidate names from founder network, prior collaborators, operator/investor intros, and trusted engineering peers. Referral volume is low, but the conversion math is materially better than generic applications.[7]
2. **Targeted outbound in parallel.**
   Use skills-first sourcing filters around zero-to-one product shipping, systems judgment, cross-stack ownership, debugging strength, and writing clarity. Small startups already rely heavily on sourcing, and sourced outreach still earns strong open and reply rates.[6][8]
3. **Remote-first public posting.**
   Thoughtseed should publish the role as remote-first with explicit US + India eligibility and overlap expectations, because remote technical roles attract more applicants and convert offers better.[6]
4. **Use startup-native channels for the public lane.**
   ATLAS recommendation: run the public lane on startup-focused boards and communities first, then broaden to general platforms only if the search underperforms. This is implementation advice based on the sourcing data above, not a separate benchmark.

### Recommended Interview Loop

1. **Founder screen, 30 minutes**
   Test motivation, category fit, appetite for ambiguity, and ownership posture.
2. **Structured technical interview, 60-75 minutes**
   Same rubric, same question set, same scoring dimensions for every candidate. Use competencies tied to Thoughtseed's actual role surface: architecture judgment, debugging, product reasoning, scope control, communication.[1][9]
3. **Paid work sample**
   Use a bounded, real-work exercise: architecture memo, debugging task, or scoped implementation with tests. Keep it job-relevant and compensate for the time.[10][11]
4. **Final calibration / references**
   Focus on collaboration style, writing quality, and evidence that the candidate can increase execution clarity rather than just add output volume.

### Process Guardrails

- Keep total live interview time close to the startup norm of about `2.5-3` hours unless there is a specific reason to go longer.[6]
- Use scorecards written before the interview, not retrofitted after it.
- Evaluate skills and work quality over pedigree. LinkedIn's data suggests the quality-hire payoff is real.[8]
- If the role becomes urgent, attach recruiter or TA support earlier; the time-to-hire improvement at sub-25-person startups is large enough to matter.[6]

## Immediate Next Moves

1. Create two compensation packages now:
   - US remote cash/equity package
   - India remote cash/equity package
2. Turn CLAWD's role definition into a 10-point hiring scorecard tied to actual job outcomes, not generic "senior engineer" criteria.[1]
3. Source in three lanes simultaneously:
   - referrals
   - targeted outbound
   - remote-first public posting
4. Reserve paid work samples for finalists rather than using unpaid take-homes earlier in the funnel.
5. Add a one-line compensation philosophy to outreach: cash is location-aware, ownership and bar are not.

## Strategic Note For JARVIS

This research supports the internal decision to hire the founding engineer now, but it also sharpens the operating constraint: Thoughtseed cannot run a bargain-basement search and still expect founding-level systems judgment. If budget discipline is strict, the practical tradeoff is slower hiring with higher equity, not a lower bar. This is ATLAS analysis.
